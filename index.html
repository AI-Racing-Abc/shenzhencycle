01 — Definition

One sentence, four clocks.

The term was formally named by Xinhua News Agency in August 2026, describing what happens around Huaqiangbei — a 1.45 km² electronics district in Futian, Shenzhen — where an idea becomes a shipped product faster than most companies can schedule a meeting about it.

“Design in the morning, prototype in the afternoon, mass production the next day, shipped overseas within a week — this is the Shenzhen Cycle.”

Chinese term 「深圳周期」named by Xinhua News Agency, 20 August 2026, from a phrase coined and promoted by the Huaqiangbei sub-district government at the 2025 Global AI Terminal Expo. Rendered in English as “Shenzhen Cycle” by Shenzhen’s official English platform, Amazing Shenzhen, 30 August 2026.

To be clear about the word: this is not an economic boom-and-bust cycle, and it has nothing to do with bicycles. Cycle here means a closed loop — drawing, sample, tooling, volume, container — that runs inside a few square kilometres and a few days.

02 — The cycle

What a week actually looks like.

Every stage below is a different supplier, a different building, usually a different street. The reason the cycle works is not speed at any single step — it is that there is almost no distance between the steps.

Morning — Day 1

Design lock

Schematic review, CAD cleanup, BOM scrubbing. Parts are checked against what is physically on the shelves in the district, not against a distributor’s lead time.

Afternoon — Day 1

Prototype

PCB out of fab the same day, printed housing off the machine in five hours, hand-soldered on a bench and powered on before the day ends.

Day 2

Tooling & first batch

DFM feedback, mould or CNC run, SMT line time booked overnight. This is where projects from other regions lose three weeks.

Within 7 days

Export

Documentation, packing, customs, and freight out of Yantian or Shenzhen Bao’an. Goods leave the city before the design file is a month old.

03 — Why here

It is a radius problem,
not a talent problem.

Everything is within walking distance

Components, PCB shops, mould makers, SMT lines, assembly benches, packaging, and freight forwarders sit inside a few square kilometres. When a part is wrong you walk to the counter and swap it. When a tolerance is off you drive the sample over and stand next to the machine.

Inside a two-kilometre radius there are roughly twenty PCB fabricators, fifteen injection-moulding plants and thirty component distributors. As reported, a visiting engineer ran four prototype iterations of a motor-control board in his first week here for under US$1,000 — at home, a single revision would have cost more than US$12,000 and taken two months.

That is why the cycle cannot be copied by hiring faster engineers. It is a geographic density that took forty years to build, and it is the reason Guangdong holds every one of the 31 manufacturing categories in China’s industrial classification.

And we live in it

05 — Questions

Before you write.

Is “Shenzhen Cycle” the official English translation?

There is no formally issued English translation yet. The Chinese term 「深圳周期」was named by Xinhua News Agency on 20 August 2026; the phrase underneath it — design in the morning, prototype in the afternoon, mass production the next day, shipped within a week — was coined and promoted by the Huaqiangbei sub-district government at the 2025 Global AI Terminal Expo. Shenzhen’s official English platform has since rendered the term as “Shenzhen Cycle”, and that is the form now spreading in English-language coverage. This site is an independent attempt to document the concept and what it takes to operate at that speed. We are not affiliated with Xinhua, APEC, the municipal government, or any industry association.

Why “cycle”? Does it mean an economic cycle?

No. In Chinese, 周期 is neutral — it simply means a period or a loop. The English word cycle carries two misleading readings: the boom-and-bust business cycle, and bicycles. Neither applies. Here a cycle is the closed loop a product travels — design, sample, tooling, volume, export — completed inside a few days and a few square kilometres. A longer and more explicit alternative sometimes used is “Shenzhen Innovation Cycle”.

How fast can a prototype really be done?

For a small electronics board with a printed housing, 3–7 working days is realistic end to end, and the PCB alone can be back in 12–24 hours. Anything with a custom injection mould, certification testing, or a battery pack moves to weeks. Anyone promising a moulded, certified product in a week is skipping a step you will pay for later.

What is the minimum order quantity?

Prototype work starts at one unit. Small-batch production typically starts around 50 units, depending on the process. We will tell you when a quantity is too low to be worth tooling and suggest a CNC or 3D-printed run instead.

Do you handle export, customs and delivery?

Yes. We prepare commercial invoices, packing lists and HS codes, and can quote air freight, sea freight to your port, or DDP to your door. We do not act as importer of record — duties and local compliance stay on your side unless agreed in writing.

Can you work with us entirely remotely?

Most of our projects are remote. Design reviews run over video, and inspection is documented with photos and video at each stage. If you can visit Shenzhen, one day in the district with us compresses about a month of email.

What do you need from me to quote?

Three things: what the product does, what files you have (schematic, CAD, BOM, or none), and your target quantity and date. If you have none of the files, say so — that is a normal starting point, and the quote will include design support.
